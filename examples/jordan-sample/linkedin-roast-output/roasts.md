# Roasts (fictional example: Jordan Sample)

Assumed: field is marketing (social media, email and events); level is early career; the job they seem to want is a marketing role built around social media and email.

Average score: 3.0 out of 10. Lowest score: 3 (all five gave a 3).

## Recruiter (3/10)

1. HEADLINE ROAST: "Passionate Team Player | Open to New Opportunities". There is no job title and no search keyword, so a search for "Marketing Coordinator" or "Social Media" will not surface this profile. "Passionate" and "team player" are claims anyone can type. "Open to new opportunities" doesn't say which opportunity.
2. ABOUT ROAST: "I am a team player with great communication skills and I am looking for a role where I can grow and make an impact." It rates itself and gives no proof. There is no number, no named result, and no brand or channel beyond "social media, email and events." Swap the name and it fits any marketer on the platform.
3. THREE WEAKEST EXPERIENCE BULLETS:
   - "Assisted with various marketing tasks as needed": "various" and "as needed" mean nothing, and it reads as filler.
   - "Worked with the team on marketing campaigns": it names no campaign, no role, and no result.
   - "Helped with the monthly email newsletter": there is no open rate, list size, or what "helped" meant. Mailchimp is in the skills list, but nothing shows what was done with it.
4. FIRST 6 SECONDS: I read the headline, find no title, and glance at "Marketing Coordinator, Bluebird Outdoor Gear." I scan the bullets for a number and find none. I don't message. Nothing separates this person from the other 99 profiles today, and the "Assisted" and "Helped" verbs suggest low ownership.
5. SCORE: 3
6. FIRST FIX: Rewrite the headline as "Marketing Coordinator | Social Media & Email | Outdoor Retail." That gets the profile into searches. Then add one metric to each Bluebird bullet.

## Hiring manager (3/10)
